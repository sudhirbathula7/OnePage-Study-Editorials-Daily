EDITORIAL 1

HEADING
Food Inflation and Rural Distress

GS PAPER
GS III

QUESTION
Why do rising food prices affect rural households severely, and why is controlling food inflation difficult?

POINT 1
Food inflation occurs when the prices of essential food items rise over time. Poorer households spend a larger share of their income on food, making price increases particularly burdensome. Rural families can face additional pressure when rising food expenses combine with uncertain agricultural earnings.

POINT 2
Food prices depend on agricultural production, weather conditions and market supply. Excessive rainfall, crop damage and changing climatic conditions can reduce the availability of vegetables and other crops. When supply falls while demand remains strong, consumers may face higher prices.

POINT 3
Food inflation can spread beyond individual commodities through transportation, processing and distribution costs. Higher fuel prices and disruptions in supply chains can make food more expensive. Such pressures may persist even when monetary authorities attempt to reduce overall demand through higher interest rates.

POINT 4
Controlling food inflation requires measures that address both immediate shortages and underlying supply problems. Better storage, efficient transportation, improved agricultural productivity and stable distribution systems can reduce price pressures. Monetary policy can support inflation control but cannot directly restore damaged crops or increase harvests.

TAKEAWAY
Food inflation places heavy pressure on vulnerable households. Lasting price stability requires stronger agricultural supply systems alongside monetary measures that manage wider inflationary pressures.

EDITORIAL 2

HEADING
Firecracker Pollution and Regulation

GS PAPER
GS III

QUESTION
Why have restrictions and cleaner firecracker alternatives struggled to prevent severe air pollution during festivals?

POINT 1
Firecrackers release smoke, gases and fine particles into the atmosphere during combustion. These emissions can worsen air quality, particularly when pollution from vehicles, construction and other sources is already high. Weather conditions that restrict pollutant dispersal can intensify the resulting pollution.

POINT 2
Green crackers are designed to reduce certain emissions compared with conventional fireworks. However, reduced emissions do not mean pollution-free celebrations. When large numbers of crackers are used together, their combined emissions can still contribute significantly to deteriorating air quality.

POINT 3
Environmental regulations become ineffective when restrictions are not consistently enforced. Illegal manufacturing, unregistered sellers and difficulty distinguishing approved products from conventional fireworks can weaken compliance. Effective regulation therefore requires reliable product identification, market monitoring and accountability throughout the supply chain.

POINT 4
Air pollution control requires action across multiple sources rather than relying only on seasonal firecracker restrictions. Cleaner transportation, dust management, industrial emission controls and public cooperation remain important. Festival-related measures must form part of a broader strategy for improving urban air quality.

TAKEAWAY
Cleaner firecrackers alone cannot prevent severe pollution. Effective air-quality protection requires enforceable standards, reduced emissions and coordinated action against multiple pollution sources.

EDITORIAL 3

HEADING
Manipur's Peace and Reconciliation

GS PAPER
GS II

QUESTION
Why does restoring lasting peace in Manipur require more than reducing violence and strengthening security arrangements?

POINT 1
Ethnic conflict can deepen when communities develop mistrust over identity, security and political representation. Violence may displace families, damage livelihoods and weaken everyday interactions between groups. Restoring order therefore requires addressing the social divisions that continue even after major clashes decline.

POINT 2
Security measures can reduce immediate violence, but they cannot independently rebuild trust between communities. Restrictions on movement and continued separation may reinforce fear and isolation. People need safe opportunities to travel, return home and resume normal activities for reconciliation to become meaningful.

POINT 3
Political dialogue is essential when communities disagree over representation, administrative arrangements and security guarantees. An inclusive process must allow affected groups to express concerns and negotiate solutions. Without credible participation, agreements may lack public acceptance and remain vulnerable to renewed tensions.

POINT 4
Long-term peace requires accountable administration, effective law enforcement and restoration of essential services. Authorities must address displacement, support livelihoods and investigate violence fairly. Confidence can gradually return when communities experience equal protection and see practical improvements in everyday security.

TAKEAWAY
Lasting peace in Manipur requires security, inclusive political dialogue, restored livelihoods and equal protection. Reducing violence is only the beginning of reconciliation.

EDITORIAL 4

HEADING
Inflation and Monetary Policy

GS PAPER
GS III

QUESTION
Why can persistent inflation lead the Reserve Bank of India to raise interest rates, and what limits the effectiveness of this response?

POINT 1
Inflation measures the general increase in prices across the economy. Headline inflation includes food and fuel prices, while core inflation excludes these volatile components. When both measures rise, it can indicate that price pressures are spreading beyond temporary changes in individual commodities.

POINT 2
The Reserve Bank of India uses monetary policy to influence borrowing costs and economic demand. Raising the policy interest rate can make loans more expensive, encouraging households and businesses to reduce borrowing and spending. Lower demand can gradually reduce pressure on prices.

POINT 3
Interest-rate increases cannot directly resolve shortages caused by poor harvests, supply disruptions or higher imported fuel costs. These supply-side pressures may keep prices elevated even when borrowing becomes expensive. Monetary tightening can also slow investment and economic growth while attempting to control inflation.

POINT 4
Inflation expectations influence how businesses set prices and how workers negotiate wages. If people expect prices to keep rising, their decisions can reinforce inflationary pressure. Central banks therefore consider future inflation risks, economic conditions and public expectations rather than responding only to the latest monthly figures.

TAKEAWAY
Monetary policy can control demand-driven inflation, but persistent price pressures also require supply-side improvements. Interest-rate decisions must balance price stability with economic growth.
