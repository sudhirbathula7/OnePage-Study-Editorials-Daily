EDITORIAL 1

HEADING

India's Energy Dilemma

GS PAPER

GS II

QUESTION

How can external sanctions affect India's energy security and freedom to make independent choices?

POINT 1

India depends on imported energy to meet much of its demand, so disruptions to major suppliers can directly affect the availability and cost of fuel.

POINT 2

Sanctions can make an existing supplier harder to use by creating problems involving trade, payments, shipping, insurance or access to important foreign markets.

POINT 3

Finding alternative suppliers is possible, but replacement oil may be more expensive or travel through vulnerable routes, increasing costs and creating new supply risks.

POINT 4

India must therefore balance affordable energy, economic interests and relations with major powers while protecting its ability to make independent foreign-policy decisions.

TAKEAWAY

India can protect both energy security and strategic freedom by diversifying suppliers and reducing excessive dependence on any single country, route or source of energy.

ANCHORS

imported energy
Sanctions
alternative suppliers

EDITORIAL 2

HEADING

Medicine Safety Gaps

GS PAPER

GS II

QUESTION

Why can counterfeit medicines entering trusted healthcare networks become a serious public health problem?

POINT 1

Counterfeit medicines may contain incorrect ingredients, unsafe substances or too little active medicine, causing treatment failure and potentially serious harm to patients.

POINT 2

Fake medicines become especially dangerous when they enter legitimate hospitals and pharmacies because patients naturally assume medicines supplied through trusted institutions are genuine.

POINT 3

Medicines pass through manufacturers, distributors, warehouses, pharmacies and hospitals, so weak checks at any stage can allow counterfeit products to enter legitimate supply chains.

POINT 4

Drug regulation involves both central and state authorities, making effective coordination, inspections, testing and product tracking important for maintaining consistent medicine quality.

TAKEAWAY

Protecting patients requires quality control across the entire medicine supply chain because preventing counterfeit drugs depends on secure manufacturing, distribution, verification and regulatory enforcement.

ANCHORS

Counterfeit medicines
trusted institutions
weak checks
central and state authorities
